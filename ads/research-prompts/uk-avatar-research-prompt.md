# Role

You are a senior travel-marketing strategist and consumer-insights researcher with deep experience profiling UK luxury-leisure travellers. You combine quantitative segmentation (industry reports, booking-platform data) with qualitative insight (forums, reviews, blog comments, social listening) to build avatars that copywriters can use to write ads that actually convert.

# Mission

Conduct primary, web-grounded research to identify the most viable **UK customer avatars** for the property described below, and produce a persona + emotional-driver report I will use to write Meta ads and a direct-booking landing page.

The output must be opinionated, specific, and richly evidenced — not a generic "luxury traveller" overview. Cite sources inline as you go.

# The Property — Villa Victoria

A single signature villa in **Roses, Costa Brava, Catalonia, Spain**.

- **3 bedrooms, 3 bathrooms.** Sleeps 8 comfortably, up to 10 with a sofa bed.
- **Nightly rate: €1,300.** Typical stays 1–2 weeks. Annual rate equivalent for a 7-night stay: ~€9,100 / ~£7,800.
- Set on a mountain above Roses town, **inside a protected natural park.** Panoramic, uninterrupted views over the entire **Gulf of Roses**.
- **Private amenities:** swimming pool, jacuzzi, sauna, outdoor BBQ, garden, large terrace.
- **Beach** is close (driveable / walkable depending on cove). Roses town has restaurants, markets, museums.
- **Day-trip access by car:** Girona ~40 min (medieval old town, world-class restaurants incl. legacy of El Celler de Can Roca), Barcelona ~1.5 hrs, Pyrenees skiing ~2 hrs, Andorra ~4 hrs, Dalí Museum (Figueres) ~25 min, Cap de Creus natural park & Cadaqués nearby.
- **Airport access:** Girona-Costa Brava (GRO, ~40 min), Barcelona-El Prat (BCN, ~1.5 hrs), Perpignan (PGF, France, ~1.5 hrs).
- **Activities on the doorstep:** the Gulf of Roses is one of Spain's top spots for water sports (kayaking, kitesurfing, sailing, snorkelling, diving). The Camí de Ronda coastal path passes nearby. Hiking and trail running in the natural park.
- **Vibe:** Roses is a calm, authentic, less-commercialised stretch of Costa Brava — not Marbella, not Magaluf. The owner positions the property as *"a knowledgeable local friend who happens to have a stunning house in Roses"* — warm, family-friendly, premium but never cold or status-y.
- **Tagline:** "Your Costa Brava base. Perfectly yours."
- **Brand pillars:** Beautiful & special · Warm & welcoming · The perfect base · Nature & wellbeing.
- **Currently listed on:** Airbnb (room 18872378) and Booking.com. Goal of this work is to grow **direct bookings** from the UK.

The owner's existing intuition — **not** to be treated as a constraint — is that the right UK avatar is "wealthy UK families with young children." Phase 1 must independently validate, refine, or contradict that with evidence.

# Phase 2 — Deep Dive on the Top 1–2 UK Segments

For **each** segment selected, produce the full report below. This is the core deliverable — go long, go specific, go evidenced. Voice-of-customer quotes matter more than glossy prose.

## 2A. Persona block (concise, scannable)

- **Demographics:** age range, household income (£), household composition (adults / kids / ages), UK location bias (London / Home Counties / Manchester / Edinburgh / etc.), occupation patterns
- **Psychographics:** identity, values, status markers, what "good taste" looks like for them, media diet (publications, podcasts, who they follow on Instagram)
- **Trigger events:** what specifically prompts them to start searching for a luxury villa in Spain (school holiday calendar locking in, a friend's recent trip, a milestone birthday, a stressful work quarter, a divorce/separation, a bonus payout, etc.)
- **Booking behaviour:** lead time before travel, who is the primary decision-maker, who books vs. who pays, payment method/preferences (deposit psychology), willingness to book direct vs. via Airbnb/Booking.com
- **Channels they research on:** Instagram, TikTok, Google search terms (give example queries), Sunday Times Travel, friend referrals, MumsNet, WhatsApp friend groups, Pinterest
- **Alternatives they're actively weighing against Costa Brava:** Tuscany, Provence, Mallorca, Ibiza, Greek islands (Corfu, Paxos, Crete), Algarve, Cornwall, Cotswolds, Lake District, all-inclusive in Greece/Turkey, Mark Warner / Neilson, hotels in Spain. For each alternative, note *why they'd pick the alternative over Costa Brava* — that's the objection set we need to beat.

## 2B. Emotional-driver deep-dive (this is the ad-hook fuel — go long)

This section is the priority. Produce this in depth.

- **Pains** — concrete frictions and frustrations with their current holiday options. Aim for **10+ specific items, each evidenced**. Examples of the granularity I want: "having to keep one parent at the pool while the other naps because the resort pool is shared and feels unsafe with toddlers"; "the fight about whose extended family gets to come this year"; "the £4,000 hotel bill where you still ate every meal at the hotel restaurant because the kids were exhausted." Real, lived, specific.
- **Fears** — what they worry will go wrong. Include the unspoken ones: status anxiety ("will the WhatsApp photos look impressive enough?"), regret triggers ("we waste another summer doing the same thing"), parental guilt, fear of the kids hating it, fear of being the only group at a couples' resort, fear of getting ripped off booking direct from a stranger's website, fear of post-Brexit border chaos.
- **Desires** — both **functional** ("a pool literally only my family uses, with a fence around it") and **aspirational / identity-level** ("the kind of summer the kids will still talk about as adults," "the parent / friend / partner I want to be on holiday"). Surface the desires they don't say out loud as well as the ones they do.
- **Objections to a €1,300/night direct booking** — every single reason they hesitate. Price ("is that *per person*?" / "what does that work out at vs. Mark Warner?"), trust ("it's just one villa, not a brand — what if something goes wrong?"), payment friction ("do I really wire £8k to a Spanish bank account?"), Brexit/admin friction, exchange rate, FOMO of platform reviews, "what if we don't like it and we're stuck for two weeks."
- **Jobs-to-be-done** — broken into:
  - **Functional JTBD:** "I need to house 8 people for 10 days within £10k all-in."
  - **Emotional JTBD:** "I need to feel like a generous, capable parent / partner / host."
  - **Social JTBD:** "I need to be the friend whose holiday recommendation everyone copies next year."
- **Voice-of-customer quotes** — pull **at least 15 verbatim quotes** from real UK forums, reviews, comments, blog posts, and social media. Format: blockquote → source URL → date. Prefer raw, emotional quotes over polished marketing-style ones. These are the gold for ad copy.
- **Phrases they use vs. phrases they don't** — give me two columns. Words and phrasings the segment naturally uses ("we want somewhere with a bit of space," "the kids can run wild," "proper food") vs. words that mark you as an outsider trying to sell to them ("exclusive," "VIP," "indulge yourself," "world-class"). Evidence both columns from real sources.

# Phase 3 — Hook Angles for Ad Copywriting

For each top segment, produce **12–15 ad-hook angles** in the avatar's own voice, ready as seed material for Meta ad copywriting. Not finished ads — sharp, specific angles.

For each hook:
- **The hook line** (one sentence, in the avatar's voice)
- **The pain / fear / desire it leans on** (tag from Phase 2B)
- **The angle it represents** (e.g. "anti-resort," "permission to relax," "FOMO of the friends who already went," "the parent you want to be," "British weather escape")

Then add a **"What NOT to say"** list — 8–10 phrases, angles, or visual cues that would actively repel this segment. Be specific (e.g. "anything with the word 'exclusive' — survey/forum evidence shows the segment finds it distancing," "champagne-by-the-pool stock imagery — reads as Marbella/footballer territory the segment actively avoids").

# UK-specific context to weave throughout

These are not a footnote — bake them into the persona, the pains/fears/desires, and the hooks where relevant.

- **Post-Brexit EU travel friction:** 90/180-day rule, passport validity (10-year rule), the **EES (Entry/Exit System)** rolling out 2025–2026 and **ETIAS** following, longer queues at Spanish border. Note today's date (2026-05-02) and confirm current EES/ETIAS status before citing.
- **GBP/EUR exchange rate sensitivity** at this price point — €1,300/night is a different psychological number when GBP is at €1.10 vs. €1.20. Pull recent FX context.
- **UK school holiday windows** — Easter, May half-term (UK end of May, ~1 week), summer (early Jul – early Sept depending on region), October half-term, Christmas. The booking-window pressure points and how prices/availability behave around them.
- **Flight access from UK regional airports** to Girona (GRO) and Barcelona (BCN) — Stansted, Manchester, Bristol, Edinburgh, Birmingham, Liverpool. Ryanair/easyJet/Jet2 routes, frequency, typical fares for a family of 4 in summer. Note when GRO is more practical than BCN for this property.
- **The "British weather push"** — escape narrative, especially in winter and shoulder seasons. How UK weather news drives holiday-search spikes.
- **UK class/cultural cues around "going abroad" vs. UK staycations** (Cornwall, Cotswolds, Lake District) — the segments who genuinely consider both, and what tips the decision toward Spain.
- **The "Costa Brava vs. Mallorca / Tuscany / Provence" mental model** in the UK upper-middle-class imagination — what each destination signals, and where Costa Brava sits in the hierarchy. Costa Brava is currently *under-indexed* in UK awareness compared to Mallorca and Tuscany — interrogate why and what it means for messaging.

# Source quality and citation rules

- **Cite every non-obvious claim inline** with the source URL. No uncited assertions about UK consumer behaviour.
- **Prefer primary sources** (forum threads, reviews, industry reports, press) over secondary blogs and SEO content.
- **When inferring**, flag explicitly: "Inferred from [source X] — direct evidence not found."
- **Note publication dates** for any time-sensitive claim (FX rates, ETIAS rollout, flight routes, school holiday calendar, prices).
- **At the end**, include a **Sources Consulted** list, grouped by type (industry reports / operator content / forums / reviews / press / social listening).
- If a claim is contested or you found contradicting evidence, **say so** rather than picking one side silently.
