# Villa Victoria, Roses — UK Customer Avatar & Emotional-Driver Report

**Executive answer.** Target two UK segments first, in this order: **(1) London/South-East dual-income professional families with primary-school children (HHI ~£150k–£300k, "HENRY" tier) booking 2-week summer or 10-night Easter villas in school holidays;** and **(2) two-family / "best-friends-since-uni" UK groups in their late 30s–40s splitting an 8-bed villa**, who deliver Villa Victoria's bullseye occupancy of 8–10. The owner's "wealthy UK families with young children" intuition is broadly **right but too narrow** — the real money is in *primary-school-aged* (5–11) not toddlers, and it is at least equally strong for *two families pooling*, which converts to higher per-night rates and more weeks per year. The most counter-intuitive insight: **Costa Brava is dramatically under-indexed in UK awareness vs Mallorca/Tuscany** — UK families consistently confuse "Roses" with the Lloret de Mar / Salou Brits-abroad strip, and the single biggest unlock for direct bookings is *positioning Roses as part of the Begur / Cadaqués / Empordà artsy-foodie cluster* rather than as a standalone destination.

## PHASE 1 — UK SEGMENT DISCOVERY

I triangulated across ABTA's *Holiday Habits 2025-26* report (n=2,001 UK adults; The Nursery Research and Planning, fieldwork 21 July–1 Aug 2025) and ABTA's *Travel Trends 2026* (published 26 Nov 2025), ~25 Mumsnet Travel/Holidays threads, Tripadvisor UK forums for Costa Brava/Roses/Begur/Cadaqués, Vrbo/Airbnb/Booking.com listings and reviews, Oliver's Travels, CV Villas, The Luxury Villa Collection, Vintage Travel, James Villas, Charming Villas Catalonia, Vita Brevis, AmaranteLVA and Villanovo's positioning copy, plus UK travel-press content (Telegraph, Sunday Times via aggregators, Conde Nast Traveller). Based on that triangulation, the five plausible UK segments for Villa Victoria are:

### Segment A — London/SE "HENRY" Dual-Income Professional Families with Primary-School Kids
- **Sizing:** ABTA finds 70% of UK adults plan an overseas trip in the next 12 months; the 25–34 cohort ("travel trendsetters") plan 5.5 holidays a year on average, with 49% planning to spend more (Travel Weekly citing ABTA *Travel Trends 2026*). Mumsnet "summer holiday budget" threads anchor this tier: HHI £230k–£350k respondents report £8k–£15k/year typical, with one £350k-HHI respondent citing "£10k summer all-inclusive" as their go-to budget.
- **Why this property fits:** Private pool (no shared loungers, naps mid-afternoon), 3 bed/3 bath layout, 8 sleeps so two siblings can each have a friend; Pyrenees-protected park location dovetails with the segment's "good food / nature / no Magaluf" identity; 25 mins to Dalí Museum scratches the "we did something cultural" parental itch.
- **Why it might NOT fit:** Three bedrooms is small if both sets of grandparents come (4–5 bed villas in Begur/Pals out-compete here); not directly walkable to a beach in the way Llafranc/Tamariu villas are — segment's blog research repeatedly cites walkable-beach as a pre-shortlist filter; mountain road may scare drivers nervous about Spanish hairpins.
- **Confidence: HIGH.** Would be raised by direct guest data (UTM/email evidence of inbound from Telegraph/Conde Nast articles).
- **Verdict: GO DEEP — Phase 2 segment #1.**

### Segment B — Two-Family / "Best-Mates" UK Friend-Group Sharing a Villa (late 30s–early 40s)
- **Sizing:** Multiple Mumsnet/Fodor's threads describe exactly this booking unit ("4 adults, 3 teens, 2 cars," "8 adults and 4 children"). The maths is brutal in their favour: at €1,300/night = £162.50/night per family of 4 — competitive with a 4★ hotel room without the kids-in-corridor problem.
- **Why this property fits:** Sleeps 8 (10 with sofa bed) is the *exact* multi-family unit; 3 bathrooms means no morning queue; private pool/jacuzzi/sauna create "private hotel" feel; Roses town has restaurants for split-decisions.
- **Why it might NOT fit:** Some friend-groups want an extra (4th) bedroom so each couple has privacy AND a spare; sofa bed for the 9th/10th person is a deal-breaker for some.
- **Confidence: HIGH.** Direct evidence from CV Villas' "Multi-generational" collection, Oliver's Travels' "school group get away" Trustpilot reviews ("really helpful to have a free 48 hour hold, when you are organising lots of people").
- **Verdict: GO DEEP — Phase 2 segment #2.**

### Segment C — Multi-Generational "Three Generations Under One Roof" (Grandparents Paying)
- **Sizing:** Recurring Mumsnet thread topic (50+ replies typical); CV Villas dedicates a whole "multi-generational" collection. AllClear Travel Insurance / 3Gem survey of 750 UK parents (July 2025) found **54% of UK grandparents have fully funded a family holiday abroad** and **40% of UK parents would turn to grandparents for help** if they couldn't afford a break themselves — material funding evidence for this segment.
- **Why this property fits:** Sauna/jacuzzi appeal to grandparents; 3 bathrooms; pool with garden = grandparents can sit out while parents drive to a beach.
- **Why it might NOT fit:** Mountain location with stairs/elevation problematic for less-mobile grandparents; only 3 bedrooms means at least one couple has to share with kids; competitor villas explicitly market "annexe for grandparents" — Villa Victoria has no annexe.
- **Confidence: MEDIUM.** Would be raised by audit of Airbnb/Booking guest data on group composition.
- **Verdict: MAYBE — secondary in marketing, not primary.**

### Segment D — Affluent Empty-Nester / Pre-Retiree Couples (55–70) on a "Slow Spain" Trip
- **Sizing:** ABTA *Travel Trends 2026* (published 26 Nov 2025, p.2) calls out the 65+ cohort showing the largest jump in September travel intention — "increasing the most from 22% to 35%, a 13-percentage point rise." Couples-only Mumsnet/Tripadvisor threads about Cadaqués and Begur are heavily older-skewing.
- **Why this property fits:** Costa Brava's Dalí + Camí de Ronda + Empordà wine + Michelin-density appeals to this segment harder than to families; off-peak (May, June, September, October) shoulder bookings unlock half-empty calendar.
- **Why it might NOT fit:** Property is sized for 8 — empty-nesters typically book 2-bed villas; €1,300/night is a stretch for 2 paying for 4 unused bedrooms.
- **Confidence: MEDIUM.** Could be a strong *shoulder-season* secondary segment if priced for 2-couple use.
- **Verdict: MAYBE — viable for May/June/Sept/Oct only, not summer.**

### Segment E — UK "Active Outdoors" Couples & Friend Groups (Cycling, Hiking, Watersports)
- **Sizing:** Niche; visible in Strava/cycling forum content and Camí de Ronda hiking blogs but small absolute volume against the family villa market.
- **Why this property fits:** Camí de Ronda 5 mins; Pyrenees skiing 2 hours; Andorra; Gulf of Roses kitesurfing; protected park hiking.
- **Why it might NOT fit:** Group of 4 cyclists won't pay €1,300/night; this is a hotel/B&B segment.
- **Confidence: LOW. Verdict: NO — don't deep-dive.**

### Phase 1 Recommendation
**Deep-dive Segments A and B.** They share a near-identical media diet, similar emotional drivers, and overlapping aesthetic ("not Marbella, not Magaluf, but not Tuscany-pretentious either"); a single Meta ad strategy targeting both with creative variations is realistic. Specifically against the owner's "wealthy UK families with young children" intuition: the data **validates the wealth tier (£150k+ HHI) and family framing, but refines the kids' age** — primary-school 5–11 not toddlers (toddlers are pool-fence-anxious and prefer half-board-plus resorts like Mark Warner Phokaia) — and **expands the unit definition** to include the two-family/best-friends group, which is likely already in the existing Airbnb book under different cover. **The owner is right; the data just shows there are two distinct booking units, not one.**

## PHASE 2 — DEEP DIVE: TOP 2 SEGMENTS

### SEGMENT A: "Lucy & James" — London HENRY Family with Primary-School Kids

#### 2A. Persona Block

- **Demographics:** Parents 38–48; HHI £150k–£300k (the bottom of "HENRY UK" — High Earner Not Rich Yet). Two kids aged 6–11. London (Clapham, Wandsworth, Islington, Hackney) and Home Counties (St Albans, Sevenoaks, Guildford); Manchester/Edinburgh secondaries. Occupations skew finance/law/tech/consulting/medicine. Mortgage £600k+; nursery just ended, school fees may be just starting; Mumsnet evidence: "we earn £120k each [...] our budget for holidays next year is going to be around £5k, increasing to £10k now we're more comfortable" (mumsnet.com summer-holiday-budget thread).
- **Psychographics:** Identifies as "well-travelled, not flashy." Reads Sunday Times Travel and Conde Nast Traveller; will pay extra for "authentic" but actively repels champagne/yacht imagery. Drives a Volvo XC60 or used Tesla Model Y, not a Range Rover. Kids in state primary OR a "soft" prep school. Good taste = Soho Farmhouse, The Newt, Cowshed; bad taste = Marbella, Sandy Lane, anything with a brand logo on the towel.
- **Trigger events:** (1) February half-term post-Christmas malaise; (2) WhatsApp group sees friends' Tuscany Instagram; (3) school PTA email about September inset days reveals a free week; (4) end-of-tax-year bonus lands in March.
- **Booking behaviour:** Plans 6–9 months ahead (research starts January for August). Mum is decision-maker on destination/villa; Dad books flights. Pays via credit card (Section 75 protection is a known concept in this cohort). **Will book direct only with strong trust signals — phone call with owner, real-name email signature, professional photography, video tour, ATOL/equivalent badge.** "I had my usual owners-direct-induced panic attack of fake villa and scamming owner the night before the holiday" is the canonical fear.
- **Channels:** Instagram (Stories, saved-collections), Pinterest boards with friends, Sunday Times Travel print + email, Conde Nast Traveller IG, Telegraph Travel (subscriber), Mumsnet Travel for crowd-sourcing, WhatsApp groups ("can anyone recommend a villa for August?"), Google searches like *"family villa costa brava private pool sleeps 8 august,"* *"villa costa brava with kids walking to beach,"* *"costa brava vs mallorca for families,"* *"is roses spain nice."*
- **Alternatives weighed against Costa Brava:**
  - **Mallorca/Pollensa** — closer in their imagination ("Soller, Pollenca" recurs on Mumsnet); they pick Mallorca over Costa Brava because *they've heard of it*. Costa Brava unlock: "fly to Girona, 40 mins to villa — closer than Pollensa transfer."
  - **Tuscany/Umbria** — aspirational ceiling. Pick Tuscany when budget hits £15k+. Unlock: "same Mediterranean light, half the price, beach in 10 mins."
  - **Provence/South of France** — older sibling of Costa Brava. Pick when they want "grown-up." Unlock: "your kids will hate the beach pebbles in Provence."
  - **Greek Islands (Corfu, Paxos, Crete)** — Mark Warner / Neilson territory. Pick when they want childcare. Unlock: "you don't need a kids' club when the kids have their own pool."
  - **Algarve (Portugal)** — strong UK family default. Unlock: "the food is materially better in Catalonia, and you don't share a pool with 200 strangers."
  - **Cornwall/Cotswolds** — sliding-doors alternative for £4k. Unlock: "30°C and a private pool vs 16°C and queueing for a roast."
  - **Mark Warner Phokaia** — half-board-plus (5 days full-board, 2 days breakfast & lunch), kids clubs and 20+ activities included; ~£8–10k for a family of 4 in peak summer per markwarner.co.uk 2026 pricing. The realistic competitor at this budget. Unlock: "no kids' club voucher politics, no eating every meal in the same hotel restaurant."
  - **Hotels in Spain (Marbella/Mallorca 5★)** — pick when they want service. Unlock: "the kids can be barefoot in their swimsuits at 4pm and not stared at."

#### 2B. Emotional-Driver Deep-Dive — Segment A

##### PAINS (specific, evidenced)
1. **"We blew £4k on a hotel in Crete and ate every meal at the hotel restaurant because the kids were exhausted by 6pm."** Pattern recurs across Mumsnet AI-debate threads.
2. **Sunbed wars at hotel pools.** "I get a large pool and that ramps up the cost" (Mumsnet) — segment will pay specifically to avoid this.
3. **Sharing a pool with 200 strangers and 50 unmasked kids.** "AND, you often get your own pool so you don't have to share it with a billion other people who are probably peeing in it!" (Mumsnet).
4. **Driving 90 min from Malaga to a "rural villa" and never being able to walk to a restaurant.** Recurring on Spain villa threads.
5. **One parent watches kids in pool while other naps, all holiday.** Recurring private-villa logic on Mumsnet.
6. **The £4,000 holiday where everything inside the resort costs €8 for an ice cream.** Mark Warner / TUI all-inclusive backlash trope.
7. **Kids don't eat the food.** "Smaller children will need some form of beige food at times bless em" (Mumsnet) — Catalan unbreaded chicken/pasta is actually a *strength* here vs. Greek squid.
8. **August 40°C heat and crying tantrums.** Recurring on Costa Brava forums.
9. **Booking a "luxury" villa via Airbnb and arriving to find the pool green.** Mumsnet/Trustpilot: "The villa was dirty, the BBQ a health hazard, the showers were rusty… The swimming pool turned green".
10. **Saturday-to-Saturday lock-in clashing with flights.** Multiple threads — flights are cheaper Tue/Wed, villas force Sat–Sat in peak.
11. **Catalan/Spanish strikes news cycle.** Charming Villas Roses review explicitly references "we were there when riots were going on in Barcelona, there was a general strike".
12. **EES/Spanish border 90-min queues post-April 2026.** Documented Ryanair complaints, Lisbon/Gran Canaria gate failures.
13. **Cost of feeding 4 humans for 14 days when not all-inclusive.** "We found if we spent less to get there, we ended up spending a lot more there" (Mumsnet, £350k HHI poster).
14. **The Camí de Ronda in flip-flops disaster.** Inferred from blog content — UK families repeatedly underestimate trail terrain.

##### FEARS
1. **Photos won't look as good on WhatsApp as Eliza's Tuscany ones.** Status anxiety; the "summer holiday Insta tax."
2. **Kids are bored by day 4** because the villa is rural with no walking access to ice cream.
3. **The £8k bank transfer is a scam.** Verbatim: "Scared we'll find out we've been scammed on arrival" (Mumsnet); "PIL were scammed out of £5k like this" (Mumsnet).
4. **EES border at Girona makes them miss a Ryanair connection.** ABTA itself has flagged this risk publicly.
5. **Stuck for 14 days if the villa is bad** — with no platform mediation for refunds on a direct booking.
6. **Brexit passport validity** — "10-year rule" trips up parents with old passports.
7. **Catalan independence flare-up** — recurring news from 2017.
8. **Driving on the right with three kids in a Skoda Kodiaq.**
9. **Kid drowns in unfenced pool.** Specific to <5s; partially relieved at 5+ but doesn't disappear.
10. **Being judged by other UK parents** for picking somewhere "Brits abroad-y." The Tripadvisor Roses thread literally asks: "We don't expect the same of Roses as long as it's not full of rowdy Brits?"

##### DESIRES — Functional and Aspirational
**Functional:**
1. A pool *only their family uses*, with a fence (or pool cover) — cited as deal-decider across Mumsnet threads.
2. 3 bedrooms so each kid has their own room and parents have privacy.
3. Walking distance to *one* good restaurant and a Condis/supermarket.
4. AC throughout — "every bedroom as well as the living room has got airco" is a real listing differentiator.
5. Wi-Fi strong enough to stream Bluey if it rains.
6. Saturday-Saturday flexibility is an irritation; willingness to pay more for date flex.
7. Hire car parking on-site (no street parking with 4 suitcases).
8. A working dishwasher and washing machine for sandy towels.

**Aspirational / Identity:**
1. **"The summer they'll talk about as adults."** Recurring trope — "memories that are worth every penny" (Mumsnet).
2. To be the family that *discovered* somewhere. Costa Brava is genuinely under-the-radar for them — that's an asset.
3. To eat actual fresh fish from a Catalan fisherman, not "fish & chips."
4. To watch their kids spend two weeks barefoot, climbing rocks, with no screens.
5. To drink an Empordà rosé on a terrace with a view at 9pm while kids sleep upstairs.
6. To feel like locals, not tourists — "live like a local for a week or two" is the recurring phrase they actually use.
7. To bring nieces/nephews and "be the host."
8. To have a holiday they can post a single, restrained, beautifully composed Instagram from — not 47 stories.

##### OBJECTIONS to €1,300/night Direct Booking
1. **"Is that per person?"** — at first glance the price reads as a per-person hotel rate.
2. **"Per family of 4 that's £162.50/night — what does that work out at vs Mark Warner Phokaia?"** Mark Warner half-board-plus at ~£8–10k for a family of 4 is the calibration anchor.
3. **"It's just one villa, not a brand — what if it falls through?"** Trust collapse; segment already burnt by Owners Direct/HomeAway.
4. **"Wire transfer to a Spanish bank account?"** Documented panic-trigger (multiple Mumsnet threads).
5. **"At €1.15/£ that's £7,924 for the week — at €1.20 it's £7,583. Should I wait?"** Real FX sensitivity; GBP/EUR was at 1.1534 on 22 April 2026, below the 2025 average of 1.1674 (per exchange-rates.org and exchangerates.org.uk: "Average exchange rate in 2025: 1.1672 EUR").
6. **"Why is it cheaper on Airbnb / Booking.com?"** Or **"why is it the same price as Airbnb if I'm booking direct?"** — segment expects a direct discount.
7. **EES/border anxiety eating into mental "ease" of the trip.**
8. **"What if we hate it on day 2?"** Two-week lock-in fear.
9. **"3 bedrooms feels small for 8 — are the bedrooms big or are we squashing two kids in?"**
10. **"It's on a mountain — can we walk to the beach?"** Walkable-beach is a primary filter for the cohort.
11. **"What if it rains?"** — Costa Brava in May/September unpredictable.
12. **"We've never heard of Roses — is that the dodgy bit of Costa Brava?"** Genuine awareness gap.

### SEGMENT B: "The Best-Mates Holiday" — Two UK Families / Friends-Group from Uni Days

#### 2A. Persona Block

- **Demographics:** Two couples, 36–46, plus 4–6 kids age 4–13. HHI of each couple £100k–£250k; combined trip wallet £8k–£15k. London + commuter belt; Manchester/Edinburgh secondaries. Likely met at uni or first jobs; have done annual or bi-annual joint holidays for 5+ years.
- **Psychographics:** Identifies as "we're easy, we just want a pool and good wine." Group dynamics matter: one couple is the *organiser* (always the same person — often a working mum in marketing/PR/HR), one couple is the *agreer*. Group WhatsApp called something like "Spain plans 🌞". Shared Google Sheet for the holiday.
- **Trigger events:** Group WhatsApp "shall we do something this summer?" message in late January / early February. New baby in one family means previous all-Greek-islands plan needs rethinking. One family did Costa Brava last year and "raved about it" — direct word-of-mouth is the segment's #1 channel.
- **Booking behaviour:** Lead time 4–7 months; one person books on behalf of the group; payment by bank transfer between friends (resolved with Splitwise after the fact); at the property they want to pay the owner via card (single transaction split internally). **Will pay direct deposit if owner provides bank details + signed contract + has been visibly active on Instagram/their own website for 12+ months.** Strong sensitivity to "who pays for groceries/maid service" frictions — the segment will pay £200–400 more for everything bundled.
- **Channels:** Word-of-mouth dominant; secondary research on Instagram (saved villa accounts), Pinterest (collaborative boards), Google ("villa costa brava sleeps 10 private pool"), Mumsnet, Oliver's Travels for "comparison shopping" (then potentially booking direct if they find the underlying villa cheaper). Will Google-image-stalk the property's surroundings on Street View before paying.
- **Alternatives weighed (different mix from Segment A):**
  - **Greek island villa (Paxos, Lefkada, Crete)** — top alternative. Pick Greek when one couple was last year. Unlock: "you're 90 mins from Barcelona for a city day; in Paxos you're trapped on the island."
  - **South of France** — pick when one couple has French A-level pretensions. Unlock: "South of France is double the price for the same swimming pool."
  - **Tuscany agriturismo with two cottages** — direct competitor for layout. Unlock: "you're not stuck in a Tuscan village, you can drive 25 mins to the Med."
  - **Croatia** — flight cost / new-and-trendy appeal. Unlock: "Croatia roads are awful with kids."
  - **Center Parcs/Eurocamp** — the budget escape if money tightens. Unlock: "this is the holiday version your kids actually deserve."
  - **Two adjacent Airbnbs** — the compromise option when no single villa sleeps 10. Unlock: "everyone in one kitchen at breakfast — you'll thank yourselves."

## UK-SPECIFIC CONTEXT (woven through, summarised)

- **EES (Entry/Exit System):** Confirmed by the European Commission (home-affairs.ec.europa.eu): *"The system became fully operational on 10 April 2026."* ABTA's consumer guidance (abta.com) adds: *"From 10 April 2026 onwards, travellers should expect EES checks to be in operation… including biometric registration such as facial image and fingerprints."* UK citizens now provide biometrics (facial scan + 4 fingerprints) on first entry, valid 3 years. Ryanair publicly called the rollout "half-baked" and reported missed connections. ABTA recommends arriving 90–120 mins earlier than usual through summer 2026. **ETIAS** is **NOT yet live** as of May 2026 — expected late 2026 (ABTA). Don't reference ETIAS as a current friction in ad copy.
- **Brexit 90/180 rule and 10-year passport rule:** Still in force; a real source of "passport check" anxiety in the segment.
- **GBP/EUR rate:** ~1.1534 as of 22 April 2026, in a tight 1.14–1.18 band. The 2025 average was 1.1674 (per exchange-rates.org; exchangerates.org.uk records the same year-average at 1.1672). At the current rate €1,300/night = **~£1,127/night**; €9,100/week = **~£7,890**. The segment will mentally compare against "8k for 7 nights." A rate of 1.20+ would push the same week to £7,583 — a £300 swing meaningful at this price point.
- **Flight access (May 2026):** Ryanair flies daily Stansted–Girona; Jet2 also operates the route; Manchester, Bristol, Edinburgh, Liverpool, Birmingham mostly via Barcelona-El Prat (BCN) on Vueling/easyJet/Jet2. GRO is materially better when staying in Roses (40 mins drive vs ~1h45m from BCN); BCN better for Begur/Pals or if combining with city. Family of 4 in summer school holidays: Stansted–Girona return ~£600–£900 with bags; BCN slightly more.
- **UK school holiday 2026 windows that matter:** Feb half-term 16–20 Feb; Easter ~30 March–10 April; May half-term 25–29 May; Summer ~23 July–1 Sept (state, England/Wales); October half-term 26–30 Oct. Private/prep schools often break up earlier (early July) and start later (mid-Sept) — **a 12-week summer window is realistic for the prep-school segment.**
- **"Costa Brava vs Mallorca/Tuscany/Provence" mental model:** Costa Brava sits below Mallorca and Tuscany in unaided UK awareness. The Costa Brava the segment *wants* is the Begur/Cadaqués/Empordà cluster — Roses is geographically and aesthetically adjacent (Cap de Creus, Aiguamolls, 25 mins to Dalí Museum) but suffers a *naming* problem: "Roses" googles into images of the Aquabrava water park / promenade, which reads more package-holiday than the segment is ready to admit owning. **The single biggest message lever is to position the property as "in the Empordà / on the Cap de Creus side of Costa Brava — yes, the same coast as Cadaqués and Begur"** rather than as "Roses."
- **Crime data flag:** Costa Brava (Girona province) was named in 2025 data as having higher-than-average burglary and theft rates per 100,000 residents (4,617 cases per 100,000). For a hilltop villa this means a low-key "alarm fitted, gated drive, on-call neighbour" trust signal, not a panic.

The owner should resist the temptation to put toddlers in lifestyle photography; primary-school-age kids photograph and convert better.

- **EES rollout is genuinely volatile** — the situation as of early May 2026 may worsen through peak summer (Ryanair publicly demanded suspension until September). Brand voice should be calm and informational, not alarmist, on this topic.
